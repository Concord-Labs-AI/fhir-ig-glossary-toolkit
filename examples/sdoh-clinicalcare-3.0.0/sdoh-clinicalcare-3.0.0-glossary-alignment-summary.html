Glossary alignment: HL7 SDOH Clinical Care IG 3.0.0: findings and work plan

This report checks every sentence of the published guide (tag 3.0.0) against its 31 glossary terms and shows, theme by theme, where the wording departs from the glossary and what the alignment work is. The companion workbook lists every finding as an exact edit for review.

How to read this report

Each finding is one place in the guide where the wording differs from the glossary, with the exact text to find and the text to put in its place. Counts are occurrences: a sentence repeated in four artifacts counts four times (68 rows are the identical 'referral recipients' → 'referral targets' edit). The review is about wording, not about whether the glossary definitions are right; where the two pull apart, that is a decision for the team (the decisions below).

  • Type. Missed use: the guide says something else where a glossary term applies. Misuse: a glossary term is used against its definition. Form: the right term in the wrong form (capitalization, hyphenation, an acronym not spelled out).
  • Status. Ready: can be reviewed now; the edit is the same whatever the team decides. Pending D-n: the edit depends on decision D-n; the row shows the strict-glossary edit and records whether the recommended option would still make it. Pending rows are not errors.
  • Confidence. High: clear-cut from the glossary text. Medium: needs a reviewer's judgment.
  • Change class. Prose: page text. Artifact text: descriptions and documentation inside profiles, CapabilityStatements, code systems and examples. Diagram: workflow-diagram source, re-rendered at build time. Identifier: an artifact's name or id; reported, never changed automatically.
  • Work-package size. S: a short session (under 25 edits in up to 3 files); M: up to about 100 edits or 10 files; L: larger, best done in bulk by rule.

D-11No system actor for the referral source and referral target0The glossary names a system actor only for the intermediary (its coordination platform). Raised independently by 8 of the 12 reading batches; no findings were written for it.

A: Add a glossary note that in technical artifacts (CapabilityStatements, diagrams, conformance statements) the role names stand for the system each organization operates

B: Define system-actor terms for both sides (e.g., referral source system, referral target system) and use them wherever a system acts

C: Leave as-is

Recommended: A — one sentence settles hundreds of sentences without rewriting normative text; B is cleaner but touches most SHALL/SHOULD statements in the CapabilityStatements.

D-12Referral definitions vs light targets and chained referrals0The Direct and Indirect referral entries require every party to have a FHIR server API, so the guide's Direct/Indirect Referral Light use cases (a referral target with only a FHIR client app) fit neither definition. A: Loosen the Direct/Indirect referral definitions to cover light targets, and add a note that source/target roles are assigned per referral

B: Add separate entries for the light variants and for chained referrals

C: Leave the definitions as written

Recommended: A — the light use cases are first-class workflows in the guide; a definition that excludes them misleads implementers.

A: Keep the glossary definition; relabel the tables' axis as LOINC-encoded vs not

B: Add LOINC encoding to the glossary definition

C: Leave as-is

Recommended: A — the glossary follows the field's meaning of 'standardized'; LOINC encoding is a separate property.

Themes

Referral actors and roles

What the glossary says. Referral source and referral target are organizational actors that each operate a system actor. An intermediary is the organization between them and typically operates a coordination platform (CP), the system actor that relays data. Direct and indirect referrals differ by whether an intermediary using a CP is involved.

What the guide does. 210 findings (164 missed use, 46 misuse): 203 ready now, waiting on a decision: 6 on D-10; 1 on D-6. Most affected: SDOHCC-CoordinationPlatform (47), SDOHCC-ReferralSource (34), SDOHCC-ReferralRecipient (27), SDOHCC-ReferralRecipientLight (25), self_referral.md (20).

  • SDOHCC-CoordinationPlatform: “Referral Recipients” → “referral targets” — Glossary names this role 'referral target'; 'referral recipient' is the retired ballot term, and the link text is also Title Case mid-sentence (the link to the Referral Recipient CapabilityStatement stays).
  • SDOHCC-PatientApp: “referral recipients” → “referral targets” — Glossary names this role 'referral target'; 'referral recipient' is the retired ballot term (link text; the linked CapabilityStatement keeps its legacy name, per D-10).
  • SDOHCC-ReferralRecipient: “referral recipients” → “referral targets” — Glossary names this role 'referral target'; 'referral recipient' is the retired ballot term.

Alignment work. Retire the role names used during balloting: 'referral recipient', bare 'recipient', 'referral requester' and paraphrases such as 'service delivery organization' become referral target or referral source, mostly in the four CapabilityStatements (the conformance artifacts that describe each system's capabilities) and on the referral workflow and self-referral pages. Also correct text copied between the source and target CapabilityStatements, and places that treat the coordination platform (a system) as the intermediary (an organization). Nearly all rows are ready now; the CapabilityStatements repeat the same documentation strings, so many rows are the same edit and can be accepted together. Where the referral source or target 'sends' or 'returns' something, the text is left alone (see D-11). (WP-1 Referral actor names (CapabilityStatements and workflow pages); size L; depends on D-10, D-11)

Organization types

What the glossary says. Social service organization is the glossary's general term for organizations that address fundamental human needs; its examples treat a community-based organization (CBO) as one kind ('a social service organization such as a CBO'). Health care organizations, public health organizations, social care networks, social care programs and accountable communities for health each have their own entries.

What the guide does. 198 findings (198 missed use): 4 ready now, waiting on a decision: 190 on D-6; 3 on D-1; 1 on D-10. Most affected: referral_workflow.md (46), capacity.md (40), self_referral.md (23), enrollment.md (16), connecting_applications_with_api_data_sources.md (11).

  • sdoh_clinical_care_scope.md: “health care, social, and public health organizations” → “health care, social service, and public health organizations” — 'Social organizations' stands for the glossary's 'social service organization', listed beside health care and public health organizations.
  • SDOHCCHealthcareServiceForReferralManagement: “a CBO” → “a social service organization” — Generic organization whose capacity is queried; the glossary's Capacity definition uses the inclusive term 'social service organization'.
  • CapacityStatusQueryLight.plantuml: “Community-Based Organization” → “Social Service Organization” — The referral target's actor label uses 'Community-Based Organization' for the generic referral target; the glossary's inclusive term is social service organization, and D-6 updates diagrams together.

Alignment work. Replace 'CBO' as the generic referral target with 'social service organization' on the workflow, capacity, self-referral and enrollment pages and in the workflow diagrams (actor boxes and step text together), keeping 'CBO' where community-based is specifically meant or in a named example. Almost every row waits on D-6. Where a SHALL, SHOULD or MAY statement names a CBO, the row is kept only when the party bound by the statement is unchanged (the referral target); four statements in the Task subscription section whose scope would widen from CBOs to all social service organizations were held out, because that is a conformance change rather than a wording change. (WP-2 Social service organization (D-6); size L; depends on D-6)

Method and limits

Every reader-visible piece of text in the 3.0.0 source was extracted (4918 text segments from 215 files; 127 files were excluded with recorded reasons, such as unpublished pages and unreferenced images). Pattern matching over the glossary terms flagged candidates; AI-assisted review passes judged each candidate in context and added paraphrases the patterns could not catch; a second, independent pass tried to disprove the judgment calls; and a final editorial pass checked every ready row and the decision tagging. Every suggested edit was then applied to a copy of the guide, which built with no new errors and one expected new warning (a changed artifact title must be registered in HL7's artifact list, which is part of the rename work). The workbook review is the human check: nothing changes in the guide until a person accepts the row. Not covered: text the publishing tool generates that is not in the source.
